The honest verdict

Nothing survives. Out of 10,996 strategies and 185,877 simulated trades, zero strategies clear the multiple-testing bar.

The best raw performer — rsi (overbought=70, oversold=20, period=2) — posts a Sharpe of 2.23 and a 43.3% CAGR. It looks like a winner until you ask the key question: out of 10,996 tries, how good should the luckiest one look?

The Deflated Sharpe Ratio answers exactly that. With 10,996 trials, pure luck alone is expected to produce a Sharpe of about 3.15 (the red line). Our best raw Sharpe is 2.23 — below what chance predicts. Its Deflated Sharpe is 0.000, i.e. there is effectively no statistical evidence its edge is real rather than the luckiest draw from 10,996 attempts.

This is the real lesson. Picking the 'most profitable of 10,000 strategies' without a multiple-testing correction is selection bias, not research. The reel this lab is modeled on shows the winner of its sweep as if the other 9,999 attempts never happened. Any strategy that 'survives' only under that framing should be treated as overfit until it proves itself on unseen, out-of-sample data — and even then, with fresh capital at risk, skepticism remains the correct default.
